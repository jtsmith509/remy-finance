## F01 — A crash during all-or-none entry loses remaining leg cash permanently

- **Severity:** high.
- **File and line:** `src/remy_finance/execution/engine.py:397`; state reconstruction at `:193`, retry guard at `:371`.
- **What breaks:** Entry leg events commit one at a time. After the first `LEG_FILLED`, `position_of()` marks every candidate leg as filled. A crash before the second event therefore leaves the entire structure open with only the first leg's premium and fees recorded. Retrying `fill_entry()` returns immediately because entry is already considered filled. Later settlement or exits process all legs against incomplete entry cash, producing wrong realized P&L.
- **Minimal reproduction:** `test_resume_entry_after_first_leg_preserves_all_leg_cash`. Create and submit the normal two-leg spread; inject a crash immediately before the second `LEG_FILLED` append; retry against the same execution board. Expected two leg fills; actual one. The failure occurs after a real first append has committed.
- **Suggested fix:** Commit the complete all-or-none entry event batch atomically. Alternatively, explicitly represent incomplete entry batches and resume exactly the missing events before exposing an open position. A successful rerun must preserve full cash and fees, not merely stop duplicating the first event.

## F02 — A crash before TRADE_CLOSED makes the outcome summary unrecoverable

- **Severity:** high.
- **File and line:** `src/remy_finance/execution/engine.py:428`; equivalent early-return paths at `:371` and `:586`.
- **What breaks:** A crash after `EXIT_FILLED`, `EXPIRATION_SETTLED`, or `ORDER_UNFILLED` but before the separate `TRADE_CLOSED` append leaves a resolved trade without its terminal summary. On retry, the early-return guards treat it as flat or entry-resolved and never write the missing close event. Scheduler loops also skip positions whose `is_open` is false. Closed-result reporting consequently loses the trade outcome.
- **Minimal reproduction:** `test_resume_after_terminal_cash_event_writes_summary`, parameterized for `exit`, `settlement`, and `unfilled`. In each case, let the terminal cash/result event commit, inject a crash at `TRADE_CLOSED`, then retry. All three leave `closed == False`.
- **Suggested fix:** Make terminal result plus close summary one atomic journal batch, or repair resolved-but-not-closed histories before the no-op guards. Recovery must cover unfilled orders and settled/exited positions, including the scheduler's discovery of unfinished work.

## F03 — Shift then clip breaks agreement between forecast mean and distribution

- **Severity:** high.
- **File and line:** `src/remy_finance/baselines/mkt1.py:371`.
- **What breaks:** `return_distribution()` shifts support values to the anchored target mean, then clips to the support's attainable return range. Clipping changes the mean again, but the recommendation still records the original anchored mean. Its quantiles, tail measures, and bin probabilities describe a different distribution. Both MKT-1 and RAND-1 inherit the inconsistent forecast, compromising calibration metrics even though selection's anchored mean remains separately protected.
- **Minimal reproduction:** `test_shift_and_clip_distribution_has_recorded_mean`. An equally weighted two-point model targeting mean 0 yields recorded atom values whose mean is approximately **+0.326173165**.
- **Realistic reproduction:** `test_realistic_skew_chain_forecast_bins_share_anchored_mean` uses the existing synthetic equity chain with 2¢ half-spreads, 1¢ ticks, and moderate skew. For the 82.5C/87.5C credit vertical, the recorded mean is **−0.60583346**, while the transformed distribution passed to binning has mean **−0.5453027243**: a **6.05 percentage-point** discrepancy. The test inspects the real binning input and compares it with the returned forecast mean.
- **Suggested fix:** Solve for a post-clipping shift whose weighted mean equals the target, validate feasibility, and derive every forecast field from the resulting distribution. Do not silently change the approved anchored mean to match an accidentally clipped distribution.
